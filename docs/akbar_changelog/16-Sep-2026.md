# 16-Sep-2026

## feature/universal-grounded-quiz-generation

1.

   - Before: Document profiling and extraction prompts were strongest for technical material and did not consistently preserve a question-sized verbatim passage across every subject.
   - After: Profiling, map extraction, consolidation, and topic rendering are explicitly subject-neutral across sciences, mathematics, humanities, commerce, technology, literature, arts, law, and interdisciplinary material, with verified source excerpts preserved for generation.

2.

   - Before: Generated questions did not retain a durable link to the source text, approximate source pages, or an evidence confidence/status that a teacher could inspect.
   - After: Migration 35 and the baseline schema store verbatim source evidence, approximate pages, grounding confidence, verification status, and warnings on each question.

3.

   - Before: A structurally valid model response could reach review even when its answer was unsupported, ambiguous, weakly explained, duplicated, or tied to invented evidence.
   - After: Deterministic checks plus one subject-neutral batch verification call assess answer shape, exact evidence membership, page provenance, explanation quality, confidence, ambiguity, and near-duplicates; failures are routed visibly to teacher review and unsupported questions cannot be approved.

4.

   - Before: The teacher review screen showed question content without the evidence needed to validate it, and True/False choices repeated words inside their badges.
   - After: Review cards show compact evidence, pages, confidence, status, and warnings; unsupported approvals are disabled, bulk approval skips unsupported questions, and True/False badges use clear symbols.

5.

   - Before: Source-quality cautions identified during document profiling were not shown to the teacher.
   - After: Direct and chunked analysis return source-quality warnings and the blueprint screen surfaces them before topic selection and generation.

6.

   - Before: Generated PDFs relied on a font/runtime path that could fail under the deployed Content Security Policy and lacked broad script glyph coverage.
   - After: PDF rendering uses locally hosted Noto Sans regular/bold fonts, preserves a substantially broader Unicode symbol set, and disables unwanted automatic hyphenation; the existing narrow `wasm-unsafe-eval` CSP allowance remains responsible for React PDF's Yoga runtime.

7.

   - Before: Grounding behavior was not regression-tested across unrelated academic disciplines.
   - After: Tests cover exact evidence and page validation across ten subject domains, batch-verifier fail-safe behavior, page-marker rendering, unsupported approval blocking, and edit-triggered re-review.

8.

   - Before: A 301-page analysis used ten AI calls, exactly exhausting the old hourly limit, while its 219,000-character consolidation request hit a hardcoded 180-second timeout; the retry was then blocked by 429 and Docker ignored the root environment override.
   - After: Large-document analysis defaults to a 30-call hourly budget, MAP/REDUCE calls have a configurable 600-second timeout, the stuck-job guard is safely longer at 900 seconds, Docker forwards all three settings, consolidation retries log their original failure, and the teacher UI clearly distinguishes consolidation from section reading.

9.

   - Before: Consolidating a 301-page document required Gemini or DeepSeek to reproduce all 106 topics and roughly 219,000 characters of extracted evidence; both providers exhausted their output-token limits even though all eight sections had been extracted successfully.
   - After: The single consolidation call returns only a compact semantic grouping plan keyed by source-topic IDs. The backend then merges the already-saved definitions, facts, formulas, examples, excerpts, and page ranges deterministically, removes exact overlap duplicates, and safely retains any source topic omitted by a malformed plan instead of losing document content.

10.

   - Before: Topic allocation used the ambiguous instruction “Assign N more questions,” and teachers could allocate beyond the selected quiz total without immediate feedback.
   - After: Empty allocation now clearly says that no questions have been assigned, partial allocation shows the number remaining, and attempts to exceed the selected total are blocked with a pop-up explaining that the total must be increased first.

11.

   - Before: Before any topic was selected, Quiz options showed the legacy whole-document question-type controls, then switched to a different total-and-topic allocation interface after selection.
   - After: Quiz options always starts with the overall question total, style, and difficulty. Question-type counts appear only inside selected topics, and generation requires at least one fully allocated topic.

12.

   - Before: Compact evidence such as `V = IR` was rejected by prose-length rules, formula questions could repeat the same learning objective with different wording, reduce-stage labels could broaden beyond the source, and thin topics could be over-allocated without guidance.
   - After: Subject-neutral evidence matching accepts equations, chemical notation, code, dates, and table values; Unicode/plain symbolic variants are matched safely; hallucinated formulas and broadened topic labels are rejected or replaced with source terms; semantic duplicate questions are blocked; derived applications are supported when all exercise inputs are explicit; large-document topics expose a suggested distinct-question limit; and generated PDFs render ordinary numeric caret exponents as superscripts.

13.

   - Before: PDF superscripts/subscripts could be detached from formulas and Symbol-font Greek letters could become Latin characters; semantic duplicate detection grouped every application of the same formula together; compact formula prefixes could incorrectly match longer formulas; and grounded rich topic/capacity checks only ran for map-reduce documents.
   - After: Character-aware PDF extraction preserves Unicode superscripts, subscripts, and standard Symbol-font Greek glyphs; formula applications are distinguished by requested target quantity while genuine repeated objectives remain blocked; symbolic evidence uses token boundaries; the AI verifier compares full-batch learning objectives; and direct/small documents now use the same evidence-verified rich topic extraction and capacity guidance as large documents.

14.

   - Before: Rich extraction could retain individual laws, formulas, events, methods, or terms only as children of a few broad parent categories, while the teacher UI exposed only the parents (for example, just Electromagnetism and Classical Mechanics).
   - After: Independently assessable child concepts are promoted to selectable topic choices across quantitative and non-quantitative subjects, focused topics remain unchanged, existing parent selections remain backward-compatible, and extraction prompts require quiz-worthy concepts to be returned as top-level topics instead of hidden under broad containers.

15.

   - Before: Generated questions could expose the answer-bearing formula in the stem, label direct recall or one-step substitution as hard, and refer to a handbook, document, or provided material that students may never have seen.
   - After: Generation and regeneration prompts require standalone student wording, hide answer-bearing rules/formulas from stems, and define hard questions by method selection and deeper reasoning; deterministic validation rejects formula giveaways, source-file references, and difficulty-label mismatches, then permits one complete corrective rewrite before failing safely. Explanations may still demonstrate the formula after the answer without referring to the teacher's file, and code remains available when a programming question genuinely requires it.
